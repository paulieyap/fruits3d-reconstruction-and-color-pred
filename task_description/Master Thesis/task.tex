\opening{}

The integration of robotic systems in agriculture presents a promising solution to several issues, including enhancing production efficiency and reducing human labor costs. These technologies offer an opportunity to automate processes like ripeness monitoring, phenotyping, and harvesting, significantly transforming traditional agricultural management. However, despite these potential benefits, several challenges remain. One of the key issues is the complexity of agricultural environments, such as crop fields or greenhouses. In these settings, the robots are often faced with occlusions from other crops, leaves, or environmental factors, making it difficult to acquire complete crop data. 

This thesis focuses on addressing these challenges that robotic systems may face while collecting data. Due to occlusions and other visual obstructions in the field or greenhouse, it is often impossible for robotic sensors to capture the entire structure of a plant or crop. This limitation impacts the system's ability to provide detailed information on crops, which is essential for effective crop management. 

This thesis will investigate the task of estimating both 3D structure and the  RGB colors of crops based on incomplete data. 
By predicting the shape 
and color of a plant from partial observations, it is possible to provide more detailed 
crop information without the need for full point cloud data, offering a more efficient 
and cost-effective solution for agricultural management. The thesis will investigate 
the use of a Conditional Generative Network for shape completion, leveraging the 
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM). This network can generate new samples from a target data used during training. This thesis will investigate the applicability 
of such models on generating a 3D shape of a crop based on partial 
shape observations, effectively "filling in" the missing parts. 
By conditioning the model on incomplete data, the system would infer 
the missing structure and RGB color information, overcoming the limitations 
posed by occlusions in traditional crop data collection methods.

The thesis will focus on 3D point clouds and partial-view RGB images of crops, s
tarting with sweet pepper plants. Building upon the approach proposed 
by Nunes et al.~\cite{nunes2024cvpr}, who applied DDPM for scene completion using 
LiDAR data, this thesis will extend their framework to agricultural applications. 
The study will adapt the DDPM model to predict both the complete 3D shape and the 
RGB colors of crops from partial observations. To achieve these results, the thesis 
will involve aligning the 3D point cloud with partial RGB data and training the model 
on this combined dataset, to achieve shape completion of crops.


\textbf{Point Of Departure}

% FIRST STEPS 

Before beginning, it is essential to conduct a thorough literature review 
and become familiar with the data that will be used. 
Magistri et al.~\cite{magistri2024arxiv} proposed a dataset for shape completion 
for sweet pepper data. This dataset will be the starting point, later extending 
the method to different fruits. The next step will involve building upon the work of 
Nunes et al.~\cite{nunes2024cvpr}, starting with the task of predicting the 
3D shape of sweet pepper point clouds using the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic 
Model (DDPM). Currently, the network operates solely on 3D point cloud data. 
The subsequent goal is to extend this by incorporating color information. 
This will be achieved by assigning RGB values to the point cloud based on 
paired point clouds and RGB images. This process will result in a new training set that 
combines both 3D and RGB information, enabling more comprehensive crop data analysis.
Finally, the method will be evaluated using the sweet pepper 
dataset \cite{magistri2024arxiv} and compared against state-of-the-art methods \cite{magistri2022ral-iros}\cite{pan2023iros}. Also, the model will be trained on further data from other varieties 
of fruits, to evaluate the proposed shape completion approach on different fruits.
