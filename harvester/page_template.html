Completing tomatoes the harvester only sees from the front

A point-cloud diffusion model (pcdiff, MinkUNet DDPM) trained to predict the whole tomato from one stereo view taken at the harvester's capture pose. Inputs are real: SAM 3 tomato masks on the wrist and stacker ZED images, lifted to 3D with their stereo depth and real colours. XYZ is the target for now; colour is only supervised where the camera saw the fruit.

Pipeline

From execution snapshots to a completion model

1 · extract

Snapshot mcap → RGB-D views

Wrist + stacker ZED colour, RVL-compressed depth, confidence and the TF chain to the world frame, one frame per camera per snapshot.

2 · segment

SAM 3, prompt "tomato"

Zero-shot instance masks. Fruits cut by the border, under 1500 px or with too little valid depth are dropped.

3 · ground truth

Procedural tomato at the real fruit

The back is never observed, so a lobed, stem-up spheroid is placed at the sphere fit of each real fruit and ray-cast through its real mask from the real camera, with ZED-like noise.

4 · train + test

pcdiff, partial-centred

Trained on the procedural GT. Tested on real wrist views against the stacker camera's view of the same fruit.

Training curves

Validation runs the full denoising loop on one fixed val fruit per epoch, so its curves are noisy and only show training health. The evaluation below uses hundreds of fruits.

Evaluation

DDPM against a sphere fitted to the same view

  • Synthetic val: held-out snapshots, procedural GT. Chamfer (mean of both directions), F-score at 2.5 and 5 mm, convex-hull volume error.
  • Real val: the stacker camera sees the same fruit from about 30° to the side. Its points that lie more than 3 mm from every wrist point are surface the wrist view never saw. "Unseen ≤5 mm" is the share of those points within 5 mm of the completion.
  • Caveat: the real metric only checks that surface the stacker saw is covered by the completion, not the other way round, so an oversized completion is not penalised. At v2 epoch 4 the real completions are about 15% larger in radius than the sphere fit (median 27.6 vs 23.9 mm), matching the +15% volume error on synthetic data.
  • Values are medians over fruits.
Checkpoints and sampler settings

Longer training samples worse

A quick scan on 24 synthetic and 8–16 real val fruits per row. With the default sampler (50 steps, guidance w = 1) the synthetic Chamfer rises from 2.9 mm at epoch 4 to 5–6.5 mm by epoch 29, and the completions shrink (volume +9% → −32%). More solver steps make it worse, not better: each step pulls the points slightly towards the centre, and later checkpoints pull harder. Guidance w = 2 pushes back and restores the size, but no setting brings a late checkpoint back to epoch 4. The in-training validation curve (8 fruits, 1000 steps, w = 2) is too noisy to show this.

Full evaluation at 100 steps (100 synthetic, 157 real fruits): epoch 4 with w = 1 drops from 2.80 to 3.46 mm Chamfer (the sphere's level) and from 1.89 to 3.01 mm on the real unseen side; epoch 29 with w = 2 reaches 3.50 mm and 3.47 mm. Both still beat the sphere on real fruits (4.58 mm), and w = 2 keeps the size right (volume −3%). Compare them in the 3D viewer below with the model selector.

Full evaluation of every 10th v2 epoch at 50 steps, w = 1 (100 synthetic, 157 real fruits; see the results table above): synthetic Chamfer 2.80 (epoch 4), 3.46 (9), 3.89 (19), 5.28 (29), 5.12 (39), 4.23 (49), 4.05 mm (59). It recovers a little after epoch 39 as the training loss keeps falling, but never back to epoch 4. On the real unseen side epochs 9 and 19 score best (1.59 and 1.69 mm, 88% and 86% within 5 mm), but treat epoch 9 with care: its completions are 18% too large on synthetic data, and the real metric does not penalise oversize. Epoch 19 is closer in size (−7%). v1 with the ε-drift fix degrades fast: 5.9 mm at epoch 9, 14.6 at 19, 28.7 at 29.

v3 is training now (results on a separate page): EMA weights, a cosine-decaying learning rate, and the best checkpoints picked by Chamfer on 48 fixed val fruits sampled like the evaluation. Until then, v2 epoch 4 at 50 steps is the model to use.

What went wrong in v1, and the fix

Why there are two runs

The first run (v1) predicts the noise ε, as the thesis code does. Its loss looked normal, but every sampled fruit landed as a small blob about 9 cm to the side. The cause: with this linear schedule ᾱ at the noisiest step is about 4·10⁻⁵, so the clean shape implied there is (x − σ·ε̂)/0.0064. A bias of 0.027 in ε̂ becomes an offset of −4σ, and later steps cannot pull it back. Re-centring the implied shape at the noisy steps brings v1 from a 72 mm to a 6.6 mm Chamfer on a quick test. That is a workaround. It still shrinks fruits, and forcing the size would erase size information.

v2 trains the same network to predict v = √ᾱ·ε − √(1−ᾱ)·x₀ instead. The implied shape stays bounded at every step, so no correction is needed. v1 was stopped after 30 epochs so v2 gets the GPU; its numbers below use the centroid fix above t = 300.

The diffusion solver also carried its history from one sampling call to the next, so every batch after the first started from the previous batch's last outputs. That is fixed in p_sample_loop. It also affects earlier evaluations made with diff_completion_pipeline.py on several batches.

Read before using the numbers

Limits of v1

  • The complete shapes are procedural. The model learns the occlusion, depth noise and colour of the real views, but its idea of the back side comes from the generator (spheroid, 0.72–0.97 height/width, up to 7 % lobing, stem dimple). There is no measured whole-fruit truth from the harvester yet.
  • The real held-out check only covers the side the stacker camera adds, not the true back.
  • The arm and stacker cameras disagree by about 9 cm in the TF tree (consistent per snapshot). Real pairs are aligned per snapshot by voting on fruit centres, then ICP; pairs without a consistent offset are skipped.
  • Training is per fruit. A truss is the set of its fruits' completions; peduncle and stems are not modelled.
