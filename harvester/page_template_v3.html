Tomato shape completion · harvester 003 · model v3

Guessing the back of a tomato from one camera view

The harvester's wrist camera only sees the front of each tomato. This model takes those visible 3D points and fills in the rest of the fruit, so we can estimate its full shape and size. This page shows how well the third version (v3) does it, and which saved version of it to use.

  • v3 is better than v2 on synthetic fruits and gets the size about right. Both beat a fitted sphere by a wide margin on real fruits.
  • It is not overfitting. On fruits the model trained on and fruits it never saw, the scores are nearly identical at every epoch (epoch 39: 3.15 vs 3.18 mm). Longer training makes both worse together, because the model learns to make fruits slightly too small. So more data would not fix it; the next fix should target size.
  • Epoch 7 is not the one to use. It was picked automatically for the lowest shape error, but its fruits are about 40 % too big. The shape error barely notices that, which is why we now look at size too.
  • 100 steps does not help. Extra steps make every fruit about 20 % smaller, so results get worse for every checkpoint except epoch 7, where the shrinking happens to cancel its oversize.
How to read the numbers

Five terms used on this page

Shape error (mm)
On synthetic fruits, where we know the true full shape: the average distance between the predicted surface and the true one ("Chamfer distance"). Lower is better. A tomato here is about 50 mm across.
Size error (%)
How much bigger (+) or smaller (−) the predicted fruit's volume is than the true one. Near 0 is best. A model can have a low shape error and still be the wrong size.
Hidden-side error (mm)
On real fruits we never see the back. A second camera (the stacker camera) sees about 30° further around the side. We check how far those extra points are from the prediction. Lower is better. It does not punish predictions that are too big.
Epoch / checkpoint
Training runs in rounds called epochs (1000 batches each). The model is saved at chosen epochs; each saved copy is a checkpoint. Later is not automatically better.
Steps
The model starts from random points and cleans them up step by step until they form a fruit. We tried 50 and 100 steps.

Baseline everywhere: a sphere fitted to the visible points. If the model can't beat a ball, it isn't useful.

50 vs 100 steps

Does more cleaning-up help?

The model turns random points into a fruit in a fixed number of steps. For image models, more steps usually means a cleaner result. Here it doesn't: with 100 steps every checkpoint makes smaller fruits (size error about 20 points lower), and the shape and real-fruit errors go up. Epoch 7 is the only exception on synthetic fruits, because it starts out 40 % too big, but it is still worse on real fruits. 50 steps is also twice as fast.

How it works

From harvester recordings to a model

1

Collect views

From the harvester's recordings we take the wrist and stacker camera images, their depth, and where each camera was.

2

Find tomatoes

SAM 3 outlines every tomato in the images. Tomatoes cut off by the image edge or too small are skipped.

3

Make training answers

We never see a real tomato's back, so for training we place a realistic synthetic tomato where each real one is and render what the camera would see.

4

Train and test

The model learns to go from the visible part to the full synthetic shape, and is then tested on real wrist views.

What changed from v2

Three fixes

v2 got worse the longer it trained, and its built-in check was too noisy to notice. v3 keeps the same model and data with three changes:

  • Averaged weights. Uses a running average of the model over its last ~1000 training batches ("EMA"), which smooths out batch-to-batch jumps.
  • Slowing down. The learning rate shrinks gradually to 1/100 of its start by the last epoch, so late training makes only small changes.
  • A better built-in check. Every 2 epochs it completes 48 fixed test fruits exactly as the final evaluation does, and keeps the 3 best checkpoints (epochs 7, 9 and 11), plus every 10th epoch.

Limits
  • The full shapes used for training are synthetic (a slightly squashed, lobed ball with a stem dip). The model's idea of the back comes from them. We have no measured whole-fruit shapes from the harvester yet.
  • The real-fruit check only covers the extra side the stacker camera sees, not the true back.
  • The wrist and stacker cameras' recorded positions disagree by about 9 cm; each pair is lined up by matching fruit centres, and pairs that don't line up are skipped.
  • Each fruit is completed on its own; stems and the truss stalk are not modelled.
